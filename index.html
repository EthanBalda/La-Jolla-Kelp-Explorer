How do ocean conditions relate to kelp?

Each dot pairs an ocean condition with one kelp observation. An anomaly is the difference from the average for that same season: positive means above average, negative means below. The zero lines mark those seasonal averages.

Pearson r measures how closely the dots follow a straight line, from −1 to +1. Values near zero indicate little linear association. These observations show association, not proof that ocean conditions caused the kelp changes. Axis ranges stay the same when timing changes.

1996 Q1 remains included as a reported zero pending team review of its conflicting source flag; it is not a confirmed kelp absence.

About the project data

This explorer loads the project's kelp, surface temperature, and regional upwelling CSVs for 1995 through 2025. The underwater scene is an illustration, not a geographical map or ecosystem simulation.

Surface temperature anomalies use the source seasonal baseline. Scatterplots compare kelp and regional upwelling with their same-season averages across the loaded record. Missing observations are excluded from paired calculations without shifting the calendar.
